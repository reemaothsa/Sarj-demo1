How it works: from a 20 ms frame to a decision

Every 20 ms two channels arrive: the agent's clean voice and the caller's microphone with everything in it. No model and no cloud service: signal processing and explainable rules, under a millisecond of compute per frame.

Remove the agent's echo

We compare the caller channel with the agent channel, estimate the echo's delay and strength every 200 ms, and subtract it.

NV-D03: speakerphone echo delayed 220 ms; the system estimated 3520 samples = 220 ms.
Ignore steady noise

A noise floor tracks the car, the AC and the street, and energy is only measured in the 250–3800 Hz speech band.

Engine rumble and mains hum below 250 Hz are not counted.
Learn the caller's voice

Their reply to the greeting gives their level in this call. After that every sound is measured relative to it.

TV is 9 dB quieter, side talk 9 dB, echo ≈ 16 dB. Real interruptions sit between −3 and +6.5 dB.
Accumulate weighted evidence

Each speech frame adds evidence according to its loudness and voicing. A car horn (frozen pitch) is rejected and its evidence removed.

STOP at ms of evidence, more careful before the voice is learned.
Words decide

With a speech recogniser attached: «لحظة، لا لا، اسمع، بس» stop at once, while «إيه، أها، صح، زين» lower the evidence weight.

With words 400 ms late: Core = 99.8 (upper bound with script words).

End of turn: adaptive patience

After 800 ms of quiet we take the turn. But «اممم…» (a nasal hum, spectral centroid ≈ 220 Hz) or a syllable held on the same pitch means "still thinking", so we wait up to 2.4 s. Each time the caller comes back after a pause, we become 250 ms more patient.

What we tried that did not work

Automatic RESUME after a short stop lowered Core from 90 to 86, because it talked over «خلاص خلاص…», which is a real interruption. Single-frame level instead of a 300 ms average: frames jump 20 dB inside the same word.

Final version: TurnSense v2

The checkpoint confirmed that v1 generalises (90.1 on 16 unseen calls vs 90.2 on dev). v2 adds one thing: a small neural speech check, so that genuine recorded noises can no longer stop the agent.

What changed from v1

  • Neural speech check. Silero VAD v6 (MIT licence, 2.3 MB, runs on the laptop CPU, no internet). While the agent talks, a frame only counts as the caller taking the floor if the VAD agrees it is human speech (probability ≥ 0.3).
  • Evidence retuned. Stop threshold 400 → 350 ms, burst gap 900 → 1200 ms, slower decay, so «لا لا … لحظة» with a pause still adds up.
  • Safe fallback. If the model cannot load, the system runs the exact v1 settings.
  • No external service. Everything runs locally.

Limits, honestly

  • v1 thresholds were chosen on only 24 synthetic calls. The organisers' checkpoint (16 unseen calls, Core 90.1) suggests they generalise; v2 was also checked once on a frozen holdout set (89.2).
  • A short lone «لحظة» and a long «إيه والله صادق، صح» are acoustically almost identical. These remain v2's errors on dev (GI-D03, BC-D02, BC-D03) and need real-time words.
  • The system assumes the caller answers the greeting. Before that it uses a more careful threshold (700 ms).
  • End of turn was measured on only two development cases.
  • These numbers come from offline evaluation. The official real-time run must be repeated before submission, and network delay counts: Core 90.2 at 20 ms and 88.1 at 150 ms.
  • The live demo on this page runs the v1 engine in the browser (the neural check needs onnxruntime, which the browser version does not load). In the live demo, replies, the agent's voice and the live transcription for the word layer come from Google Gemini; the stop / take-turn decisions are TurnSense's own, made from your audio and those words.
